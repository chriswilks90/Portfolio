UX Research UI Design Web Redesign

Uniqlo
Redesign

Redesigning Uniqlo's homepage for desktop users. The existing mobile-first experience left desktop shoppers confused, frustrated, and ready to leave.

Role Lead UX Researcher & UI Designer
Type Academic Project
Duration Jul to Sep 2023
Deliverable Homepage Redesign
uniqlo.com

Project Overview

A mobile-first site that
failed desktop shoppers.

Uniqlo's website was built with mobile in mind, but the majority of considered purchase decisions happen on desktop. The result was a confusing, friction-heavy experience: gender categories that looked clickable but weren't, a choppy single-scroll layout, and no dropdown navigation to help users find what they came for.

My goal was to audit the existing experience, conduct user research, and redesign the homepage to better serve Uniqlo's core demographic. Gen Z and Millennials make up the majority of online shoppers and they deserved better.

Core Objectives

  • 01 Fix confusing gender category navigation with clearly functional controls
  • 02 Add hover-activated category dropdowns expected by desktop users
  • 03 Redesign the homepage layout to properly utilize desktop screen real estate
  • 04 Reduce friction between landing and product browsing for high-intent shoppers

User Research

9 interviews.
One clear picture.

Each participant was asked to navigate the live Uniqlo website and attempt to purchase a shirt. The results confirmed what the design audit suggested. The desktop experience was broken in consistent, fixable ways.

100%

felt the site was optimized for mobile, not desktop

90%

wanted a faster, simpler way to find and browse products

80%

were confused by gender category buttons that appeared clickable but weren't

67%

found the single-scroll landing page choppy and disorienting on desktop

Design Challenges

Four problems to
solve by design.

01

Broken Navigation

Gender category buttons looked interactive but went nowhere. It was the single most-cited pain point across all 9 interviews and the first thing every user noticed.

02

No Dropdown Menus

Desktop users expect hover-activated category dropdowns. The mobile-ported nav offered none, forcing users to hunt for products with no clear browsing path.

03

Poor Layout Scaling

The single-scroll homepage was built for a phone. On a wide screen it felt awkward, with content that was too small, oddly stretched, or frustratingly slow to scroll through.

04

Missed Audience

Gen Z and Millennials are 25.3% more likely to shop online than average, yet the desktop experience didn't come close to meeting their baseline expectations.

Design Process

Four phases.
Two months of work.

Discover

Design audit of the existing Uniqlo site and competitive analysis of comparable e-commerce desktop experiences.

→
Define

9 user interviews, synthesis of key pain points, and feature prioritization to align on what to fix first.

→
Develop

Concept exploration, wireframes, and a full desktop-first homepage redesign addressing every major pain point.

→
Deliver

User testing, feedback collection, and iteration cycles to reach a final polished design.

Design Solutions

Two fixes that
changed the experience.

Functional Category Navigation

Replaced the non-functional gender category buttons with clearly labeled, clickable navigation that routes users directly to the right section. This eliminated the single most-reported point of confusion across all 9 interviews.

Hover-Activated Dropdown Menus

Added category dropdowns that activate on hover. This is the standard desktop nav pattern that 90% of users expected and the original site completely lacked. Now users can browse directly from the nav without hunting through the page.

Outcome

A desktop experience
Uniqlo shoppers deserved.

The redesign addressed every major pain point surfaced in research. Broken navigation was fixed, standard desktop patterns were added, and the layout was restructured to actually serve the screen it was being viewed on. The result is a homepage that respects how Gen Z and Millennial shoppers browse: quickly, confidently, and on a large screen.
